# Narration for the 2-minute demo video.
#
# Each scene is "@ id | shot note" followed by its spoken text; the id matches a
# renderer in scripts/video_scenes.py. scripts/make_video.py renders one clip per
# scene and times that scene's animation to the measured clip length, so a line
# can be rewritten and re-recorded without touching the rest of the film.
#
# Written to be HEARD, not read: contractions, short sentences, varied openings,
# and numbers spelled the way they should be spoken ("two and a half angstroms",
# not "2.5 Å"). Say "the Nano", never "the box".

@ meta | tooling card -- the voice reading this is itself the evidence
Quick thing first. We thought it'd be funny to make this video on the Nano too.
So this voice, the music, every frame -- all of it came off that machine.
Obviously.

@ hook | Rosie, then the 75% counting up
In twenty twenty-four, a dog named Rosie was dying of cancer. Her owner isn't a
biologist -- he's a machine learning engineer. He had her tumour sequenced, used
AI to find the mutations that mattered, and designed her a vaccine. Her biggest
tumour shrank by three quarters.

@ question | eight cloud steps stacking up against one
So why can't every hospital do that? Here's the catch. The input is a human
genome. It identifies the patient, and partly their family. In most places it
can't legally leave the building. Sending it out doesn't cost a network hop. It
costs weeks.

@ problem | 1,890 dots dimming to the fifty that matter
And the hard part was never making the vaccine. It's choosing what goes in it.
One tumour throws up about nineteen hundred candidate fragments. Maybe fifty are
real. In the largest trial ever run, of the best candidates anyone could
nominate, six percent worked.

@ build | the funnel, actually funnelling
That's what NeoFold Edge does. It screens all nineteen hundred in nine seconds.
Cuts anything matching a healthy human protein. Folds the survivors in three
dimensions on the GPU. Then explains every one in plain English.

@ wow | rows arrive anonymous, then the surround dims away
Watch this one. It comes from KIT, a real cancer driver. By every conventional
measure it ranked first. It's also an exact match to a healthy human protein.
Target that, and you attack the patient. Our filter caught it.

@ nano | four model chips, memory bar, watts
Four models, all live at once. Three neural networks and a physics engine. That
takes a hundred and twenty-eight gigabytes of unified memory, and thirty-eight
watts. About what a laptop pulls.

@ proof | 2.5 against 2.7, then the thirteen-minute bar
Does it work? We predicted one key atomic contact at two and a half angstroms.
The crystal says two point seven. A whole tumour takes about thirteen minutes.

@ close | tagline and repo
A cancer research lab that fits on a desk. And never phones home.
