# Narration for the 2-minute demo video.
#
# Lines starting with '#' are comments. Each scene is "@ id | shot note" followed
# by its spoken text. scripts/make_video.py renders one audio clip per scene and
# times the visuals to the measured clip length, so scenes can be re-recorded
# individually without rebuilding the whole film.
#
# Written for Piper, so numbers and units are spelled the way they should be
# SPOKEN, not the way they are written in the docs: "two and a half angstroms",
# not "2.5 Å".

@ hook | title card, then the Rosie facts as type
In twenty twenty-four, a dog named Rosie was dying of cancer. Her owner is a
machine learning engineer, not a biologist. He had her tumour sequenced, used
A.I. to find the mutations that mattered, and designed her a vaccine. Her
largest tumour shrank by three quarters.

@ question | governance diagram: cloud path versus edge path
So why can't every hospital do this? Because the input is a human genome. It
identifies the patient, and it partly identifies their family. In most places,
it cannot legally leave the building.

@ problem | five gates diagram, then the base-rate card
And the hard part was never making the vaccine. It is choosing what goes in it.
One tumour throws up about nineteen hundred candidate fragments. Only a handful
are real. In the largest trial ever run, six percent worked.

@ build | pipeline diagram, then the live dashboard
NeoFold Edge does that choosing, entirely offline, on one HP ZGX Nano. It
screens all nineteen hundred in nine seconds. Cuts anything matching a healthy
human protein. Folds the survivors in three dimensions. Stress-tests them with
physics. Then explains each one in plain English.

@ wow | SLOW ZOOM onto the flagged row in the candidate table
This is why that second step matters. This candidate comes from KIT, a real
cancer driver. By every conventional measure, it ranked first. It is also an
exact match to a healthy human protein. A treatment aimed at it would attack
the patient. Our filter caught it.

@ nano | hardware diagram
Three neural networks and a physics engine, all resident at once. That needs a
hundred and twenty-eight gigabytes of unified memory, and it draws thirty-eight
watts. No rack. No cloud. Nothing leaves the room.

@ proof | structure panel with the contact readout, then the run timeline
Is it right? We predict one key atomic contact at two and a half angstroms. The
crystal structure measures two point seven. One tumour takes about thirteen
minutes.

@ close | closing card: tagline and repo
A cancer research lab that fits on a desk, and never phones home.
