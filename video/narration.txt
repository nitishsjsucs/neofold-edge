# Narration for the demo video.
#
# Each scene is "@ id | shot note" followed by its spoken text; the id matches a
# renderer in scripts/video_scenes.py. make_video.py records one clip per scene
# and times that scene's animation to the measured clip length.
#
# TWO HARD BUDGETS, both from research/research-demo-video.md:
#   1. ~290 words. At Amy's measured 158 wpm that is under two minutes. Every
#      new sentence has to evict an old one.
#   2. Readable characters on screen, not spoken words, is the binding limit:
#      about 20 characters per second (Netflix's subtitle ceiling). A frame
#      nobody can finish reading is not doing any work.
#
# ORDER MATTERS. `what` comes before `hook`: a viewer who stops at 45 seconds
# should already know what this is. Leading with Rosie alone means they leave
# having watched a dog-cancer documentary.
#
# Written to be HEARD: contractions, short sentences, varied openings, numbers
# spelled as spoken. Say "the Nano", never "the box".

@ meta | pre-roll joke, deliberately short
Quick thing: we made this whole video on the Nano. The voice, the music, every
frame.

@ what | THE ONE-LINER. what it is, in one sentence, before anything else
NeoFold Edge takes a tumour's DNA and picks the targets a cancer vaccine should
aim at. It runs on one small computer, completely offline.

@ hook | Rosie, then the 75% counting up
Here's why that matters. In twenty twenty-four, a dog named Rosie was dying of
cancer. Her owner isn't a biologist. He's an engineer. He had her tumour
sequenced, used AI to pick the targets, and designed her a vaccine. Her biggest
tumour shrank by three quarters.

@ question | eight cloud steps stacking up against one
So why can't every hospital do that? The input is a human genome. It identifies
the patient, and their family. In most places it can't legally leave the
building. Sending it out costs weeks.

@ problem | 1,890 dots dimming to the fifty that matter
And the hard part was never making the vaccine. It's choosing what goes in it.
One tumour gives about nineteen hundred candidates. Maybe fifty are real. In the
biggest trial ever run, six percent worked.

@ build | the funnel, actually funnelling
So here's what it does. Screens all nineteen hundred in nine seconds. Cuts
anything matching a healthy human protein. Folds the survivors in three
dimensions on the GPU. Then explains every one in plain English.

@ wow | rows arrive anonymous, then the surround dims away
Watch this one. It's from KIT, a real cancer driver. By every conventional
measure it ranked first. It's also an exact match to a healthy human protein.
Target that, and you attack the patient.

@ nano | four model chips, memory bar, watts
Four models live at once. Three neural networks and a physics engine. A hundred
and twenty-eight gigabytes of unified memory. Thirty-eight watts.

@ proof | 2.5 against 2.7, then the thirteen-minute bar
Does it work? We predicted a key atomic contact at two and a half angstroms. The
crystal says two point seven. A whole tumour takes thirteen minutes.

@ close | tagline and repo, and nothing else
A cancer research lab that fits on a desk. And never phones home.
