How to read DAI, and why it is not a filter DAI is the germline peptide's predicted binding divided by the mutant's, damped per Łuksza et al. 2017. Read it together with Site. A mutation at an anchor (P2 or PΩ) changes MHC binding, so it produces a large DAI without necessarily changing what a T-cell sees — anchors point down into the groove. A TCR-facing mutation changes the surface the receptor reads while leaving binding roughly unchanged, so a DAI near 1 is expected and is not evidence against it.

We measured this rather than argued it: on 1,947 pairs DAI scores AUC 0.660 on anchor mutations and 0.577 on TCR-facing ones — the exact inverse of presentation (0.719 / 0.801). It is an anchor detector. Gating on it discarded TCR-facing candidates and promoted anchor mutants, which is backwards. Presentation is the only gate.

How sure the model is about each residue's own position. The bands and thresholds are AlphaFold's. The hues are too, but lightened: AlphaFold's exact values are calibrated for a white page, and on this dark one Very high would measure 2.6:1 against Low at 12.6:1 — making the band that means "trust this" the hardest to see. Predicted aligned error
Error expected in the position of each residue pair. Dark is good. The square blocks are the three chains; the thin bright bands are the flexible chain termini. The question this answers that pLDDT cannot: is the peptide placed confidently relative to the groove — measured mean 2.2 Å peptide↔HLA, against 1.7 Å within the HLA itself.

This is not a distance map and not a contact map, and it is asymmetric: the value at (x, y) is the error expected in residue x's position when the two structures are superposed on residue y. The ramp is clipped at 8 Å; AlphaFold DB clips its own at an "arbitrary cut-off of 31 Å" for the same reason.
Predicted structure, not an experimental observation. Confidence describes the model's self-assessment, not biological validity — we measured that a deliberately mismatched peptide/allele pair still scores ipTM 0.988, and structure predictors are known to dock non-binding peptides into the groove (Motmaen et al., PNAS 2023). Ranking comes from the binding screen.

Do not read binding strength off the picture. For a published wild-type/mutant pair (PDB 7L1B / 7L1C), a 70× difference in complex half-life corresponds to just 0.73 Å of peptide backbone RMSD. Binding lives in anchor chemistry and kinetics, which a static image cannot show — which is why this panel measures one named, pre-specified contact instead.
